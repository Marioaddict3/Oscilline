# Renderer

The window is an SDL3 renderer. Lines are expanded on the CPU into triangles and drawn with `SDL_RenderGeometry`, so the same path works on the software renderer and on a GPU. There is no depth buffer. Segments are sorted far to near.

The logical view is 640×480 at 4:3, 853×480 at 16:9, and tracks the drawable width at the selected unrestricted aspect ratio. SDL letterboxes the logical view onto the window without stretching geometry. The window is created with `SDL_WINDOW_HIGH_PIXEL_DENSITY`, so the backbuffer follows the display scale while gameplay coordinates stay in the logical view.

The loop samples a fixed 60 Hz step. `FixedStep` latches the first timestamp, then reports how many steps elapsed and the leftover fraction in `[0, 1)`. A stall is capped so the simulation does not fast-forward. `oscilline --frames N` still exits after N presented frames, including frames spent on the loading bar, and does not pace itself, which keeps the dummy-driver smoke test short.

## Wireframe

TMD lines become one segment. Untextured triangles and quads become their edges. Rectangle packets stay skipped: psx-spx marks that body as unknown, and the parser only keeps the raw bytes.

Two conversions live in `tmd_wireframe` and are the ones to change if a disc disagrees with them:

- `TmdObject::scale` is a bit shift applied before the pose. Positive shifts left, negative shifts right, and the shift is clamped to 16.
- ANM rotation is Euler **Rx, then Ry, then Rz**. Radians are `value * 2π / 4096`. Scale is `value / 4096`. Position is the raw int16. An object with no key in the current frame is not drawn. Frame interpolation blends position, scale, and the short way around each angle, and it does not blend a hidden object into view.

The second ANM header field is documented as unused. The viewer treats the observed values 1, 10, 15, 20, 30, and 60 as a playback rate in frames per second, and uses 30 otherwise.

The default camera sits on +Z and looks at the origin with a perspective divide. Endpoints on the near side of the camera are dropped. The model viewer orbits that framed camera (left-drag, or Q/E and R/F) and zooms with the wheel or `-`/`=`. P restores the framed distance and a zero orbit. Tab opens the SFX browser; Tab returns to the model or camera view. Tests project with an orthographic camera so the expected pixels are exact. Placeholder strokes stay white. A disc model can copy packet colors; the placeholder path does not.

`tmd_wireframe` still turns a polygon into its edges. The course figure uses `tmd_figure_mesh` instead: line primitives stay strokes, and polygon primitives are filled triangles in packet color (a quad is `(0,1,2)` and `(0,2,3)`). Those fills share the far-to-near sort with the figure's lines. Play-form polygons are black occluders (`0x000000` or `0x010000`). Course strokes (ribbon, obstacles, figure) use a 1.0 logical-pixel core and 0.5 px of feather per side. Text and the HUD stay on the 2 px stroke. The ribbon is one line; `--ribbon-guides` adds the 24 px ticks, the hit marker, and the perfect and good edges of each obstacle's window. Obstacle outlines live in `course/shapes.cpp`. The arch uses `kArchHeightToWidth` (1.21) and `kArchWidthFraction` (0.544), so it is taller than it is wide, and it is seated on its left edge, where the perfect window ends. The pit is a narrow V (`kSpikeHalfWidthFraction` 0.22). The loop keeps its cross above the ribbon and its circle above the cross, open between the cross intersections. The cross arms cover `kLoopCrossSpanScale` (0.75) of the previous vertical span and the feet are `kLoopCrossFootScale` (1.5) times as far apart, and it is seated on the left ribbon foot (`kLoopPerfectCenterFraction`); the perfect window ends at the circle's front edge. The wave is a zig-zag (`kWaveWidthFraction` 0.96) with four peaks above the ribbon and three below. The six pairs are those parts merged: vertical-sided blocks with a pit or wave roof, a block with a circle on top as tall as the block, a loop with a crown spike, a pit with spiked sides, and a spiked loop whose spikes join the circle with no separating chord and which keeps the loop's cross.

## Model view

TMD and ANM use PlayStation axes: +X right, +Y down, +Z away from the viewer. After the pose, `to_view_space` rotates into the renderer, which is +Y up with the camera on +Z. `Side` (the default) maps `(x, y, z)` to `(z, −y, x)`, so the model's +Z points to screen right and the camera sits on the model's +X side. `Front` maps to `(x, −y, −z)`. Both are rotations (determinant +1). The viewer builds one perspective camera from the posed bounds of every animation frame, so the figure stays in view, and `--view side|front` selects the mapping. Gameplay uses the same side mapping and plants the idle clip's feet on the ribbon, then lifts the drawn model by `kFigureRibbonClearancePx` (2 logical px) so the stroke sits on top of the line. The same clearance is applied to the placeholder. `--anc <name>` draws the eye and target polylines in full, and an eye-to-target segment on the first key, every Nth key, and the last key, framed on both, in the same view. N defaults to `max(1, round((key_count − 1) / 50))`, so a long path shows about 50 looks. `--anc-step N` sets that stride (`1` draws every key). `--disc-camera` is the gameplay default, not a viewer mode. A course with script sections follows the per-section road files in [disc-map.md](disc-map.md). With no spans, the intro is `ROAD/TV_SS.ANC` at 30 keys per second from course start, then the first key of `ROAD/S01.ANC`. The ribbon and obstacles take the unwrapped road angle. A disc figure is projected through that camera (eye, target, no roll) at the origin facing `+Z`, at `kFigureWorldPerModel` (0.71) world units per model unit, so a vertical model line stays screen-vertical. Projection distance `H` is `kAncProjectionScale / fov` (`250000 / fov`). The S01 play key (fov 499) keeps `H` about 501. The raw channel is not `H`. Orientation is high confidence. Mid-intro scale and the anchor between keys about 105–135 are medium: 0.71 is a 49-row rabbit in the 286-row frame (original 49 rows, p10–p90 44–54), about 82 logical px against the side view's 88. A placeholder figure stays upright and only scales. Screen x is not mirrored. The center is (256, 124) in a 512×286 PAL frame. `--builtin-camera` keeps the built-in path, apart from the thinner course stroke and the filled figure polygons. `--no-disc-assets` stays on the built-in path. Custom music with no disc, or when the user picked the built-in camera, stays on that path. Custom music with a disc and the disc camera uses the disc camera. Gold courses and a gold custom chart on that camera add a screen shift estimated from video; see [disc-map.md](disc-map.md).

`oscilline-viewer --disc <cue>` mounts the image, refuses an unknown boot file, and reads `GAME/02_FILES.PAK`, then `GAME/01_FILES.PAK` on the Japanese disc (Experimental), then `GAME/FILES.PAK`. `--pak` selects one archive. `--tmd` and `--anm` select entries by the name stored in the PAK.
