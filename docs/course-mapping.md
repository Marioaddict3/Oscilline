# Course mapping

`oscilline --disc <cue>` reads `SCRIPT/SYSTEM3.FSL` from the language archive (`GAME/02_FILES.PAK`, then `GAME/01_FILES.PAK` on the Japanese disc (Experimental), then `GAME/FILES.PAK`). Play Original on the title opens a difficulty selector when a disc is loaded and disc assets are on. Bronze plays courses 1–2, silver 3–4, and gold 5–6, back to back. `--course N` still plays one course. The window labels it "Course N". It does not print a song title. Play My Music, or `--music <file>`, charts a WAV, FLAC, or MP3 instead of a disc course. That chart is one course. The window labels it with the file name.

The parser in `fsl.cpp` only reads the file. Every decision about what an obstacle is, when it is hit, and which button clears it lives in `src/course/mapping.cpp`, `src/course/obstacle.cpp`, and `src/course/play.cpp`. Change those, not the parser, when a real disc disagrees with this note.

## Known

These match published format notes, the manual, and the CD layout. They are not guesses.

- Obstacle ids are 0 block, 1 pit, 2 loop, 3 wave, 4 block+pit, 5 block+loop, 6 block+wave, 7 pit+loop, 8 pit+wave, 9 loop+wave.
- Pattern type 0 is a break, 1 is a fixed list, 2 is a distribution. A distribution stores cumulative buckets: the chance of id `i` is `(prob[i] - prob[i-1]) / maxProb`, and `prob[-1]` is 0.
- Speed and shadow at time `t` in a control segment are `base + (t - start) * delta / 4096`. A larger speed value is a slower obstacle. The shadow period is the minimum gap between obstacles.
- Course index `i` plays CD track `i + 2`. Track 1 is the data track. Published listings of this disc name the audio as track 2 Polaroid, 3 Sunny Day, 4 Laugh & Peace, 5 Universal Dance, 6 Overflowing Emotions, 7 Roll Along, and 8 the kiosk audio. `--course 1` is the first of those. The picker offers courses 1 through 6. FSL table entry 7 is a ~31-minute progression table. It is not a course and it does not drive custom-music mode.
- Buttons: left shoulder (L1) block, right shoulder (R1) loop, d-pad down pit, south face (X) wave. A pair is both buttons. On the keyboard those are Q, E, S or Down, and X or Space.
- A CD-DA sector is 2352 bytes, 588 stereo frames, 16-bit little-endian, left then right, played at 44100 Hz. Obstacle timing follows that playback clock.
- FSL time values use 22050 units per second. Segment times, the shadow period, and the speed value are all in that unit. `kDefaultUnitsPerSecond` is 22050. One unit is `1000 / 22050` milliseconds.
- The public notes say the custom-CD table stores no timings, so obstacle placement can follow the music. On the built-in courses the speed value, read as a period, tracks each song's beat period. For most courses it is the beat; on one it is half a beat; on a course whose tempo changes, it ramps with the tempo.
- Base shadows are often a whole number of speed periods or a little over one. Many are far shorter than one speed period.
- The distribution `count` field does not behave like a count. Its low byte is constant and the other bytes look packed or unused.

## Observed

Black-box readings of emulator outputs and RAM values, not a disassembly. Seeds were checked on all six courses (on 2, 4 and 6 from the state words in RAM, traced back through the generator). Type order was checked on courses 1 and 3, plus the fixed sections that open course 5.

- The generator is `state = state * 1664525 + 1013904223` in uint32, the same constants as before.
- The roll is `uint32((uint64(state) * uint32(maxProb)) >> 32)`. The smallest `i` with `roll < prob[i]` wins. A remainder of `maxProb` uses the low bits, which alternate, so a 50:50 section becomes strict alternation.
- A random section starts the state at `uint32(random_seed) * course_number`. `course_number` is 1-based (track index + 1). Course 1 with seed 11 starts at 11. Course 2 with seed 4 starts at 8. Course 3 with seed 311 starts at 933. Course 4 with seed 4 starts at 16. Course 6 with seeds 603 and 602 starts at 3618 and 3612. So it is not `2 * tier + 1`, which would give 4 on course 2, 12 on course 4 and 3015 on course 6.
- Each section whose record differs from the last reseeds from its own record. Course 1's second record (seed 8) starts at 8.
- The generator steps once per obstacle in hit order. The attack at about 0.5 s on courses 1 and 3 uses the first step (the state steps about 1.5 s after it, long before the next attack can commit), so on disc courses no lead-in cut applies. Obstacles past the audio end take no step. Course 3 keeps one state across its three sections that share a record.
- Course 5 opens with fixed sections. During them no seeded state exists or steps; that slot holds the fixed list and its cursor. A fixed obstacle takes no step.

### Hit times on disc courses

Black-box readings: emulator frames, the game's CD position, and RAM values read as data while courses 1, 3 and 5 played. Only derived numbers are kept here; the captures and logs are not in this repository.

- The original charts every disc course from the CD audio while it plays. With a silent track 2, course 1 spawns no obstacles at all. The script supplies speed, shadow, and pattern, never a hit time.
- The game copies the CD audio from the SPU capture buffer in 256-frame chunks and advances its course clock 128 units per chunk (22050 units per second).
- The emphasis is Ps / Pl of the mono mix (L + R) / 2. Ps, the mean square over the last 1024 frames, matches the short energy the game keeps for each potential attack. Pl is the mean square over the last 8192 frames; the game's long energy is 1-2.6% away from that, and its exact form is not known. This matches the short and long blocks in the public patent US 2001/0048762 A1.
- Selection, from the game's analysis state and its hit flag watched over whole courses:
  - Select windows are laid back to back from 0. Each is as long as the script shadow value (FSL field 2) and offers its largest emphasis as a potential attack.
  - The first potential at least two speed periods after the last attack becomes the pending attack, however small its emphasis.
  - A later potential more than 1.5 times larger replaces the pending attack, as long as it comes before the pending attack's commit time (the attack plus two speed periods). Every logged replacement had a ratio of at least 1.52.
  - At the first window end at or after the commit time, the pending attack becomes an obstacle.
- The obstacle collides with the figure at its audible attack. The obstacle center passes x 93.75 of 800 0.625 speed periods later.
- The course runs until the music ends. Breaks (pattern type 0) place none.
- PCSX-Redux is not usable for this: it delivers about one capture chunk per frame instead of 3.45, so the game there sees a third of the audio. DuckStation runs were used instead. Two runs from the same savestate gave the same hit times. Between savestates, the game's chunk phase against the music moves by up to about 20 ms (+19 ms in the course 1 run).

Runs of one base type are 1 to 4 long, the sequence does not strictly alternate, and a long enough chart uses all four base types. Audio may nudge about 12 percent of the choices. Pairs are mixed from the start, about 30 percent, and energy or onset strength can move that share a little (`kPairActivitySwing` 0.10, clamped to 20–45 percent); busy commercial tracks land near 0.33. When a later beat-aligned window matches an earlier onset envelope, the chart copies that phrase of 4 to 8 obstacles. A tone with no real onset starts as an even loop grid, then those midpoints move onto that period's quarter-beat grid. A re-encode keeps the hit times and can change the types. |
| Music scores | `music_score_path` | The best total for a file is keyed by a 32-digit hash of the decoded samples and stored in the user config directory, never in the repository. A truncated long file hashes the kept prefix. |

Custom-music placement. High-confidence rows are the default. Each medium row has its own constant, and `--gen-experimental` turns on every constant that is still false. Promoting one row is changing that constant to true. Disc courses do not read these. The loudness gate, the noise bed, and long silences were promoted after a per-track comparison with the original on the disc's audio tracks and synthetic probes. Track density and the beat stride stay opt-in: they bring the density and gaps close to the original, but their slot count follows the onset rate, so a re-encode that adds or drops onsets moves the hits.

Judgment uses the audio clock directly. The picture interpolates between the last two clock samples so the ribbon does not step. If the audio device cannot be opened, the clock falls back to `SDL_GetTicks` and a warning is printed. Reads keep at most 15 minutes of a track (`kMaxCddaSeconds`).

The placeholder on the ribbon is an original stick figure. Its forms are super, rabbit, frog, worm, and out. Drawn feet sit `kFigureRibbonClearancePx` (2 logical px) above the ribbon center. Obstacle pictures are original outlines: a tall narrow arch, a narrow pit, a loop with an open circle above its cross, an angular wave, and the six pair silhouettes. Nothing is traced from a disc model.
